## D199 — The minutes wheel is a clock with hours: H:MM:SS, one shape on every dosed run form, stacked above or below the miles wheel

**Finding.** Two shapes satisfy V184 D2 (three columns max), V182 D4 (dash on the left column only) and V184 D5 (a column wraps exactly when it has no dash): a two-column MMM:SS with a long non-wrapping minutes column, or a three-column H:MM:SS. The bounds question decides it. The free dimension on dist forms (the whole long-run family: Long Run, LSD, Peak, RACE DAY, 45,360 forms) runs 6.82..268.55 min at target pace and real finishes run slower, so a two-column minutes column either refuses a real marathon finish (anything capped near the engine's 268 or the 26.2 × 11:17 cross-product of 296) or becomes a 300 to 600 row throw from the dash, which is the 1,000-row miles column Mario already rejected in V182 D2. A clock with hours bounds itself: hours 0..9 covers every finish under 10:00:00, which is the file's one existing ceiling on logged cardio minutes (`max="600"` on the rest-day sheet, index.html:1402), and the engine's longest implied time (4:28:33) sits well inside it. Every column is then a short throw: hours at most 10 rows, minutes and seconds at most 30 via wrap. That is V182 D5 honoured (bound derived from a table the file already carries, not chosen) and D2's ergonomics honoured.

**Coaching argument.** Time on feet is spoken in hours and minutes once it passes an hour ("two and a half hours", the NRC marathon plan's own "Two Hour Run"), and hours is the grain the long-run tiers key on (≥75 min, 45–75 min). A watch readout past an hour reads H:MM:SS. The clock should carry the digit the athlete is thinking in. The price is that 74.5% of dosed entries are sub-hour (80,010 of 107,460 at target) and read `0:15:00` with a hours column parked on zero. Accepted: a stopwatch shows that digit too, and one shape across time and dist forms beats two faces for one quantity.

**Spec (derived, not chosen).** New `_IAW_SPEC` kind, `fmt` a three-column clock: `cols:[{min:0,max:9,nil:true},{min:0,max:59,pad:2},{min:0,max:59,pad:2}]`, cap `Time`. Hours carries the dash and does not wrap; minutes and seconds wrap (D5, derived from `nil`). No carry between columns (D5). Face `h:mm:ss`, hours unpadded.

**Layout consequence (V184 D2).** The dosed forms today put the two fields side by side in `.log-row`. Two three-column wheels side by side are six columns, which V184 D2 measured as under the 44pt target at 375pt. The wheels therefore stack: fixed dimension first (the one the strip prints), free dimension second, matching today's left-to-right order. Form detail for the session: a `max-width` on each stacked wheel so a three-column wheel alone in its row keeps cells near the generic form's measured width rather than sprawling to ~109pt. DOM weight: time wheel 11 + 300 + 300 = 611 rows, plus the miles wheel 201, about 810 wheel rows per dosed form against ~480 on the generic form (handoff 1693 watch item). `_IAW_REPS` stays 5; it remains the dial if Mario's device stutters. This is his to check on first use; no gate measures it.

**Deliberately not changed.** Pace and rep-time kinds; the generic run form's five-column row; no fourth column anywhere.

**Mario's call (it is his program and his thumb).** Recommendation: H:MM:SS. Counter: MMM:SS keeps the dash on the column every entry must touch and lets a sub-hour run be two touches instead of three, but it cannot hold a marathon without a column longer than the one he already rejected.

## D200 — Stored minutes stay decimal, emitted to two places, and two places round-trip every second

**Finding.** The session decided storage stays decimal minutes (no reader, gate or stored log changes). Coaching has no objection: every reader in measure A's list (`doseDerived` 13460, Benchmark 13043, persist 14664, `_hasLog` 14100, g191) reads decimal minutes already. The ruling here is the seam's precision. Emit `h*60 + m + s/60` to two decimals (`7:30` → `7.50`, `15:20` → `15.33`, `0:30:00` → `30.00`), mirroring V184 D1 which fixed two decimals as the emission grain for the sibling miles field. Two decimals is exact at the one-second grain: the rounding error is at most 0.005 min = 0.3 s, under half a second, so re-seeding `round((stored - whole) × 60)` lands on the second the athlete left the wheel on, and the fraction never carries into the next minute (59 s → .98). Parse accepts a bare integer, a decimal, and a leading-dot decimal (D203), never requires a colon; stored values above 599.99 clamp hours to 9 for display only.

**Requirement that is a gate, not a preference.** `iaWheelInit` seeding writes nothing (V182: reopening a day never rewrites what was logged). A stored `''` must still read `''` after init on every dosed form, including a form whose fixed wheel shows the planned face (D202).

Recommendation: two decimals. Counter: four decimals loses nothing at all, but it prints `15.3333` into any export or history view that shows raw minutes and buys no face the athlete can see.

## D202 — A blank fixed wheel shows the plan; a blank free wheel shows the dash

**Fixed dimension (time-form minutes, dist-form miles).** Blank storage on these fields already means "as planned" in every reader: `doseDerived` substitutes `dose.mins` / `dose.mi` and prints "assuming planned …"; on dist forms the V148 stamp writes the planned miles into `run_dist` on the first input. So a wheel face showing the planned value over `''` storage shows exactly what `''` means on that form. V182 D3's forbidden state was the reverse: an entry the ATHLETE made (9:30) that the app had not stored and would drop. Here nothing the athlete entered is unstored, and the derived line still says "assuming planned" until a column moves, which tells the athlete the number is the plan, not a measurement. Seeding to the plan is display only (D200's gate). The first settle on any column commits the whole face as a number, including when it lands back on the planned value; from then on the derived line drops "assuming" because the athlete confirmed it. "Didn't log it" (D4) stays reachable: on the time wheel the dash is one notch below 0 hours; on the miles wheel it is the row above 0. On the fixed dimension dash and plan are the same stored state, so the dash is "clear back to as planned", nothing is lost.

**Free dimension (time-form miles, dist-form minutes, reps_time miles).** Parks on the dash. An implied value at target pace exists on most forms, but `''` on the free field means "nothing measured", `doseDerived` returns `{}` and the derived line is blank. A face reading 47:30 over `''` here IS the D3 trap: it looks logged, derives nothing, and a Done tap stores nothing. The coaching reason is stronger than the engineering one: the prescription owns the fixed dimension and the log captures the free one. Pre-filling the free field with the target would bias every record toward "hit target" and defeat the log. Benchmark and Retest (no target) become a non-case under this rule.

**The one cost, named.** With the dash on hours, a sub-hour free time entry is three touches (hours off the dash to 0, minutes, seconds), and the half-set face `— : 47 : 23` stores `''`. That state class already exists on the dist wheel (`— . 8 6` stores `''`, measure A F3) and the blank derived line is the cue. D206 adds one line of copy under the free time wheel to defuse it. Pulling hours off the dash when another column moves was considered and rejected: it is a cross-column write (V184 D5) and it makes the dash harder to reach, not easier.

**Mario's call.** Recommendation: plan on the fixed wheel, dash on the free wheel. Counter: dash everywhere is the purest D3 reading and one convention on both wheels, but it turns every one of his recovery runs into a throw from zero when the plan is right there in the strip.

## D203 — One legacy-seeding defect is fixed; the rest are accepted display clamps

Stored values typed into today's number boxes seed the wheels through `_iawParse`. Rule per measure A F3:

- **Fix:** a leading-dot decimal (`.86`) lands on the dash, and a touch of any column then commits `''`, erasing a legitimate log. `.86` is a legal value from an iOS numeric keypad and every reader parses it as 0.86. The `dec3` regex takes an optional whole (`^(\d*)(?:\.(\d)(\d)?)?`, empty whole reads 0); the new minutes parse accepts the same shape. This is a parse-seam change, no storage change.
- **Accepted (display-only clamp, V182 wording "clamps for display only"):** `3.456` shows 3.45 (thousandths are not a watch grain; V184 D1 fixed hundredths); `100`/`150` miles clamp to 99 (no run is 100 miles; garbage in); `-1`, `1e1`, `3,5` land on the dash or a truncated whole (not real watch values; iOS comma behaviour is measure A's open unknown and cannot be ruled here); decimal minutes like `15.333` land on the nearest second (15:20), which is the seam doing its job; minutes `100`/`150`/`185` are moot under D199 (`1:40:00`, `2:30:00`, `3:05:00`, exact).
- The current clock regex's "15 of 16 decimal strings land on the dash" is moot: the minutes kind parses decimals (D200), never requires a colon.

Recommendation: fix the leading dot only. Counter: rounding `3.456` to 3.46 is more correct than truncating, but no watch prints a thousandth and the extra branch defends a value nobody logs.
